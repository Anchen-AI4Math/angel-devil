We first show that every finite nonempty side-connected set can be constructed from one square by adding a square sharing a side with the existing set. A path from inside a proper subset to outside it supplies a crossing edge; termination is by the number of missing squares. A new square has at most three exposed sides and removes at least one old exposed side. Therefore perimeter increases by at most two. Induction from the singleton perimeter four proves the bound. Holes are included in the perimeter count. The proof does not assume simple connectivity or a prior spanning-tree theorem.
